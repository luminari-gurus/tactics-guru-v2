Combat UI — fewer steps

The shipped battle HUD (issue #108 design, #111 rail/dock) makes every move and attack a four-control ritual: pick a verb, pick a target (often twice), Review, then Confirm. These mockups keep preview-before-commit and 44px targets, and cut the redundant chrome. Items stay out of combat, as they do today.

Current UI Recreation of the live rail/dock. Move 3 taps · Attack 4 · Wait 1. A · Board + wheel Direct manipulation. Confirm on the tile. Move 2 · Attack 2. B · Bar + defaults Chosen. Persistent Go. Chevron picker kept for comparison. Move 1–2 · Attack 1–2 · special 2, +1 to switch. C · Radial hold Hold/release gestures. Move 1 · Attack 1. Harder to discover.

Option B · special-selection alternatives

Chevron stays. These three replace the ▾ / long-press picker with different ways to choose among multiple specials, still without an Ability dropdown.

B · Target-first Tap an enemy. The bar lists only specials legal for that target. Last-used valid special is already pressed. Recommended. B · Segmented row Every owned special is a first-class bar button. Greyed in place. One special → one button. B · On-board chips After a target tap, ability chips with forecast sit next to that unit. No special slot in the bar.

What is redundant today

  • Ability dropdown and the Signature button bind the same owned ability.
  • Target dropdown duplicates board taps (and keyboard cell select).
  • “Selected (x, y)”, target-info, ability-info, availability copy, button subtitles, preview, and the Confirm dialog all restate the same decision.
  • Review action plus a Confirm modal is two confirms. Wait needs none.
  • Confirm dialog also offers Restart battle.
  • Board heading, round/turn line, and the legend caption compete for the same status.
  • Inspect party restates the actor panel. Inspect target is a third stats surface.
  • Portrait HUD on mobile is capped at 56% of the viewport, so the board is a strip.

Recommendation

Keep Option B’s bar + Go + path chip. Replace the chevron picker with target-first specials: tap an enemy (or tile), the bar shows only specials that currently preview as legal, last-used valid special is preselected, Go commits. Default special stays 2 steps; another legal special is one extra tap on that button. A production hero with one special sees one pressed button and nothing else to pick. Keyboard: 1/2… pick a listed special, S cycles the valid list, Enter = Go.

Segmented is the runner-up if the catalog stays at two or three specials and you want switching to cost the same as tapping Attack (ability-first, range highlights before a target). Board chips match Option A’s on-tile language but occlude isometric cells. Leave the chevron page in the gallery; do not ship the dropdown. Swipe-the-slot is out. Treat Option C’s hold as a later mobile extra, not the only way to act.
